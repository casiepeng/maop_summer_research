### Understanding "Sensor" Data in Gorilla Tag VR's Privacy Policy

The Gorilla Tag VR privacy policy (as of my review) **does not explicitly state that it collects raw, real-time sensor data** like accelerometer readings, gyroscope data, or direct eye-tracking information from your VR headset.

However, it does collect information *about your device* that implicitly relies on the existence of sensors for the device to function.

Let's break down what's collected:

---

#### 1. What's Collected: Your "Headset Type"

The policy states under **"Information We Collect Automatically"** that it collects **"Device Information."** This includes:
*   IP address
*   Operating system
*   Hardware
*   Screen resolution
*   Browser type
*   Language
*   **Headset type**
*   Other information about your computer or mobile device and Internet connection.

    <strong>We know:</strong> "You're using an Oculus Quest 2" or "a Valve Index."
</p>
<p align="center">
    This tells them the *model* of your VR headset, not how you're moving it around in detail.
</p>

---

#### 2. What's NOT Explicitly Collected (Raw Sensor Data)

The policy **does not mention collecting granular, real-time data** from the various sensors *within* your VR headset (like accelerometers for head movement, gyroscopes for rotation, or cameras used for inside-out tracking).

<p align="center">

</p>

#### Key Takeaway

While your VR headset is packed with sensors, the Gorilla Tag VR privacy policy focuses on collecting your **headset's model or type** for operational and analytical purposes, rather than harvesting raw, real-time movement or environmental data directly from those internal sensors.
